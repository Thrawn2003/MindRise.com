Approach

How we think about learning

Five ideas, drawn from decades of research on memory, that every Mindrise tool is built to respect.

Chapter I

Begin with attention

Learning starts before the material does. It starts with whether you can actually look at it, free of the small interruptions that quietly spend your mind. A page that is calm is not a luxury; it is the first piece of teaching equipment.

Working memory is small. When an interface asks you to decode itself, it is borrowing from the same account you need for the idea in front of you. We call that cognitive load, and we treat it as a budget.

Mindrise software never counts your points. Only your understanding is worth measuring.
Chapter II

Retrieval over rereading

Rereading feels productive because the page looks familiar. Familiarity, however, is not knowledge. The reliable way to strengthen a memory is to pull it out of your head, which researchers call active recall.

Recall asks the question first and shows the answer after. That small reversal is most of the method.

It feels harder than rereading. That is the sign that it is working.
Chapter III

Let time do some of the work

Memories fade quickly at first and then more slowly. Each time you retrieve something, the fading slows down. Reviewing at growing gaps, a practice known as spaced repetition, uses that to your advantage.

The curves here are schematic, not measurements from any one learner.
Chapter IV

Mix things up, deliberately

Practising one kind of problem over and over feels smooth, and teaches you less than it seems to. Mixing related topics, or interleaving, is slower and more effortful, and it trains the skill that exams actually test: choosing the right idea.

Effort of this kind is what Robert Bjork named a desirable difficulty. Our tools add it carefully, and never so much that it turns into frustration.

Difficulty is only desirable when it can be overcome.
Chapter V

Rest is part of the work

Memory is consolidated away from the desk: in pauses, on walks, and above all in sleep. A good study session ends while there is still some energy left, and a good tool lets you stop without guilt.

That is why Cadence suggests breaks and never counts streaks, and why none of our products will ever send you a reminder that makes you feel behind.
