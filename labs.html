Labs

Small experiments you can play with

Four ideas from science and maths, each one a living background for this whole site. Press a button and the page itself becomes the experiment.

  1. 01.

    Murmuration

    Complexity
    Starling flocks turn as one with no leader. In 1986 Craig Reynolds showed that three simple rules are enough to imitate it: steer away from crowding, match the heading of your neighbours, and move toward their centre.
    The flock is curious about your cursor.
  2. 02.

    Game of Life

    Computation
    John Conway’s 1970 “game” has two rules. A live cell with two or three live neighbours survives, and a dead cell with exactly three comes alive. From that come gliders, oscillators and endless surprises: a classic first look at emergence.
    Move across the board to bring cells to life. Click to drop a glider or a restless little pattern.
  3. 03.

    Harmonograph

    Mathematics
    A harmonograph draws with pendulums. Two or more fading oscillations at slightly different frequencies add up to loops and rosettes. Here the frequencies drift slowly, so the drawing keeps changing shape.
    Move left and right, up and down, to change the frequency ratios.
  4. 04.

    Ripple tank

    Waves
    Waves from several sources overlap. Where crest meets crest they reinforce, and where crest meets trough they cancel. This interference is behind noise-cancelling headphones and the colours of a soap bubble.
    Your cursor is the third wave source.

These and the other backgrounds live behind the Background button at the bottom right of every page. Press B to move through them.
